If you have looked at website quotes in Australia, you have probably seen numbers that feel miles apart. That is usually not “someone is ripping you off”—it is **different definitions of scope**.

## What you are actually buying

A website purchase is typically a bundle of:

- **Structure and layout** (information architecture, mobile UX)
- **Visual polish** (spacing, typography, component consistency)
- **Performance hygiene** (fast loads, sensible images)
- **SEO fundamentals** (metadata, headings, internal linking—not magic guarantees)
- **Integrations** (forms, analytics, booking widgets—sometimes simple, sometimes not)

If one quote assumes “five pages + enquiry form” and another assumes “memberships + payments + automations”, the price gap makes sense.

## The practical drivers of price

### Content readiness

Copy and photos ready to go speeds everything up. Starting from zero is fine—it just needs time budgeted.

### Integrations

A basic contact form is straightforward. Deep CRM pipelines, custom quoting engines or bespoke ecommerce rules are not.

### Timeline pressure

Rushed timelines often mean parallel workstreams and fewer optimisation passes.

## A sane buying approach

Ask for **deliverables**, not vibes:

- What pages are included?
- Who owns domain and hosting accounts?
- What happens after launch—training, documentation, support?
